Atatepe — Turkey's Largest Atatürk Statue

Artvin Centre

About 3 km from the centre, Atatepe is home to Turkey's largest Atatürk statue and gives a bird's-eye view of the Çoruh Valley and the town. Run by the municipality, it serves breakfast and food facing the view and has an indoor area. Take a minibus from the bus station to the centre, the free municipal shuttle at weekends, or a taxi.

Hatila Valley Glass Terrace

Artvin Centre

Inside Hatila Valley National Park, about 12 km from the centre, the Glass Terrace sits at 490 m, some 220 m above the valley floor. No overshoes are needed. The entry fee is paid at the national park gate about 4 km before the terrace (October 2026: 190 TRY per vehicle, with receipt). There is no public transport; take a taxi from the bus station or the centre.

Artvin (Livane) Castle & Kaledibi Marina

Artvin Centre

Perched on a rocky hill, Artvin (Livane) Castle is closed to visitors for safety reasons but can be seen from many points in town. One of the best spots is Kaledibi Marina, run by the provincial administration right on the Çoruh River, where you can watch the castle and the river over tea or coffee.

Kafkasör Plateau & City Forest

Artvin Centre

Just 8 km from the centre at 1,250 m, Kafkasör Plateau is famous for its traditional bullfights held every year. Its picnic areas in cool forest are open all year round. The road continues through the city forest to Yankı Hill, Atabarı and Genya. No minibus: go by taxi or car.

Yankı Hill (Echo Hill)

Artvin Centre

On the road from Kafkasör to Atabarı, about 1 km before Mersivan Plateau, this wooden viewpoint opens onto the forest. Its name, 'Echo Hill', comes from the echo you hear when you shout towards the opposite slopes. Go by taxi or car.

Atabarı Ski Centre

Artvin Centre

About 18 km from the centre on Mersivan Plateau at roughly 1,800 m, Atabarı is home to Artvin's only ski slope. A chairlift climbs to 2,054 m and the 1,300 m forest run is open November to May. It lies on the same road as Kafkasör and Yankı Hill. It comes alive in winter, when the road can be snowy.

Genya Hill

Artvin Centre

On the same route as Kafkasör, Yankı Hill and Atabarı, Genya Hill rises above 2,000 m a little beyond Mersivan Plateau. It is worth the drive on a clear summer day. In winter, late autumn and on rainy days in March-April the road can be snowy.

Deriner Dam Viewing Terrace

Artvin Centre

At km 13 of the Artvin–Ardanuç/Şavşat road, on top of the dam body, this terrace overlooks the reservoir, the mountains and Artvin town. Tiered terraces offer picnic spots and a café. At 249 m, Deriner is Turkey's second-highest dam. It is often windy, so don't plan a long stay in cold weather. District minibuses pass by, but a taxi or car is recommended.

FAQ

What are the best places to visit in Artvin?

Highlights include Atatepe, the Hatila Valley Glass Terrace, Kafkasör Plateau, the Deriner Dam viewing terrace, Karagöl–Sahara National Park, İşhan Church, the Cehennem Deresi Canyon and the Macahel Biosphere Reserve. This guide lists 57 places across 9 districts.

How much is the Hatila Glass Terrace entry fee?

As of October 2026 it is 190 TRY per vehicle, with a receipt, paid at the national park gate about 4 km before the terrace. Prices may change.

How many days do I need for Artvin?

Allow at least 4-5 days to see all districts. One or two days are enough for the centre and its surroundings; the Yusufeli, Şavşat and Borçka highlands need separate days.

When is the best time to visit Artvin?

June to September: the plateaus are green, roads are open and the weather is good. Atabarı is for skiing in winter, when high roads can be snowy.
